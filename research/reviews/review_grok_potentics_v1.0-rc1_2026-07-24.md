---

## 1. Summary of the thesis

Potentics is proposed as a transdisciplinary research program for the rational exploration of The Possible. It distinguishes three levels: The Possible (what relevant constraints have not forbidden), a possible (a sufficiently specified candidate state), and a visible possibility (what particular actors currently perceive). A potentiality is defined as a conditional, graded, revisable assessment of a possible’s path to actualization (not a probability, not a pure capacity, not a value judgment).  

The program introduces a multidimensional potentiality profile Π (propensity φ, effort ε, value υ, reversibility ρ, plus domain-specific dimensions) and a second-order epistemic metapotentiality Π_e (the value of knowing whether Π is real before committing). It treats designed, discriminating failures as epistemically valuable, provides a provisional taxonomy of inhibitors (resistance to change, visibility failures, coordination failures, resource/timing mismatches, epistemic failures), and insists on the “primacy of the real” as continuous mutual constraint between model and practice.  

A minimal reusable inquiry record is specified, three illustrative designs are offered, and concrete conditions under which the program itself would fail are stated. The philosophical root is Possibilism; the direct intellectual ancestor is cybernetics. The paper is explicitly incomplete and presents itself as a first foundation open to empirical test and revision.

## 2. Symmetry test

The document is a high-fidelity **source document**.  

The central thesis, the three-level distinction (The Possible / possible / visible possibility), the definition of potentiality as graded conditional assessment, the structure of Π, the five inhibitor categories, the primacy of the real, the minimal record, and the falsifiability conditions can all be reconstructed from the text alone without external knowledge of the author’s other projects.  

Minor asymmetries: the illustrative examples lean on the author’s known practical interests, but they remain intelligible as generic demonstrators. Overall symmetry is strong.

## 3. Stabilized concepts

The following concepts are already stable enough for reuse in derived products:

- The three-level distinction: **The Possible / a possible / a visible possibility**.
- Potentiality as a *conditional, graded, revisable assessment of a path to actualization* (explicitly distinguished from probability, capacity, value, and preference).
- The primacy of the real (model valid only if practice confirms it; practice intelligible only if model illuminates it).
- Epistemic value of failure *conditional on a discriminating and documented trial*.
- The minimal Potentic record (eight elements).
- The five inhibitor categories as a provisional but usable taxonomy.
- Π_e as the value of a discriminating inquiry prior to full commitment.

These can travel into one-pagers, methodology notes, or domain applications without immediate collapse.

---

## 4. Fragile or ambiguous concepts

- **Π as profile vs. future scalar**. The paper correctly retreats from premature unification, yet the notation Π(p) = f(φ, ε, υ, ρ, …) and the long-term ambition of a “single comparable index” keep the scalar temptation alive. Independent users may still treat the profile as if a number already existed.
- **Propensity (φ)**. Defined as “intrinsic tendency … absent deliberate intervention.” The boundary between “intrinsic” and “already shaped by past interventions or structural conditions” is not yet operational. How does one measure or even estimate φ independently of ε?
- **Value (υ)**. Explicitly stakeholder-dependent, yet the profile still treats it as one dimension among others. Conflict of values is acknowledged but not given a formal place inside the profile or the record.
- **Learning rate**. Introduced as a property of the potentiality itself; useful intuition, but almost no operational proxy is offered.
- **“Relevant constraints”** that define The Possible. The list is open-ended. Who decides relevance, and under what procedure of contestation?
- **Designed failure**. The conditions for strong epistemic yield are stated, but the practical threshold between a “well-designed investigation” and ordinary project post-mortem remains soft.

These require either tighter operational definitions or explicit warning labels when the concepts are reused.

---

## 5. Conceptual drift risks

- **Possibility ↔ Potentiality ↔ Probability**. Despite careful separation, everyday language and the historical baggage of Popperian propensity invite slippage.
- **Inhibitor → Excuse**. The taxonomy is meant to be diagnostic; it can easily become a post-hoc rationalization that protects the original hypothesis rather than testing it.
- **Primacy of the real → Pure empiricism**. The productive tension is correctly stated; the risk is that “practice confirms” is later read as “whatever worked is therefore theoretically validated.”
- **Π_e → Rationalization of delay**. The epistemic metapotentiality can be invoked to justify endless investigation and never committing to actualization.
- **Science of the Possible → Philosophy of the Possible**. The title and the concluding invitation create a soft boundary between research program and philosophical stance (Possibilism).

---

## 6. Strong objections

### 6.1 Accuracy with respect to the state of the art (insisted)

The paper’s central claim of a practical gap is overstated relative to neighbouring traditions.

- Real-options theory already treats unexploited possibilities as time-dependent assets.
- Adaptive management, design science research, action research, and certain strands of strategic foresight already institutionalize co-production of knowing and doing and the design of discriminating probes.
- Innovation studies and socio-technical transition frameworks already map inhibitors.
- Second-order cybernetics and viable system models have long asked “what could the system become?” under constraint.

The paper correctly lists these traditions and claims only an integrating role. However, the rhetoric of “the gap is real” still reads as stronger than the comparative table supports. A more accurate formulation would be: “these traditions address fragments; no single, lightweight, cross-domain, auditable record for situated possibles has achieved wide adoption.” Without tighter comparative analysis, the claim of a distinct research program remains vulnerable to the charge of re-description.

### 6.2 Operationality of the distinctions (insisted)

The three-level distinction and the definition of potentiality are conceptually clean. Their operationality for independent investigators is not yet demonstrated.

- Two different researchers examining the same project can easily disagree on whether a given constraint is “relevant,” on the boundary between visible possibility and latent possible, or on the magnitude of φ versus ε.
- The inhibitor taxonomy is useful as a checklist but does not yet contain decision rules or diagnostic tests that would force inter-rater convergence.
- The minimal record is a strong step toward operationality, yet it still lacks guidance on *how* to fill each field under time or information pressure, and on how to handle irreducible value conflict inside υ.

Until inter-investigator reliability data or at least worked examples with contested fillings exist, the distinctions remain more lexical than operational.

### 6.3 Solidity of the conditions of refutation (insisted)

Section 7.3 is one of the strongest parts of the paper. The five conditions under which Potentics would fail as a distinct program are concrete and testable in principle.  

Remaining weaknesses:

- The conditions are still relatively high-level (“adds no predictive, diagnostic, or coordination value beyond ordinary project management”). What constitutes “ordinary”? What is the threshold of “no value”?
- No positive success criteria are stated with equal force. The bar for continued work is low.
- The program’s own falsifiability is therefore asymmetric: it is easier to keep the program alive than to kill it.

A stronger version would specify observable failure metrics (e.g., after N independent applications in domain D, the record does not reduce the rate of costly false commitments relative to a control set).

### 6.4 Level of ambition of the title (insisted)

The title “What is Potentics? Toward a Science of the Possible” carries a high degree of ambition.  

The body of the paper is more modest and repeatedly undercuts the ambition (Π not unified, measurement unsolved, first foundation only). At present the answer is: **borderline disproportionate**. “Science of the Possible” evokes a mature, measurable, cumulative discipline. What is offered is a well-articulated research program and a candidate inquiry protocol. Titles such as “Potentics: A Candidate Protocol for the Rational Exploration of Situated Possibles” or “Toward Potentics” would better match the actual epistemic status of the document. Retaining the current title is defensible only if the paper is explicitly framed as a programmatic manifesto rather than a first scientific paper.

## 7. Signal/noise report

**Integrate now**  
- Explicit comparative sharpening of the “gap” claim against real-options, adaptive management, and design science.  
- Stronger operational guidance (or explicit provisional status) for estimating φ independently of ε.  
- Symmetric success criteria to match the falsification conditions.  
- Title or subtitle adjustment (or a clearer framing sentence that the title is programmatic).

**Keep as piste**  
- Future work on a general theory of inhibitor interaction.  
- Mathematical formalization attempts (with the explicit caveat of the primacy of the real).  
- Cross-domain calibration of learning rates.

**Reformulate before integration**  
- The long-term scalar ambition for Π (keep the research objective but mark it more clearly as distant and possibly unreachable).  
- The list of “relevant constraints” (make the contestability of relevance itself part of the minimal record).

**Ignore as noise**  
- Minor stylistic variations in the illustrative examples.  
- Further expansion of the philosophical lineage beyond Aristotle, Popper, Peirce, Dewey, Wiener.

**Requires human arbitration**  
- Whether to keep, soften, or change the title.  
- How much comparative literature is required before claiming a distinct program.  
- Whether value conflicts should receive a dedicated slot inside the Π profile or remain external.
