# Baron Mariani — historical title, devolution and institutional identity

## Purpose

Separate four questions which must not be conflated:

1. historical existence and creation of the title;
2. genealogical and legal devolution of the title;
3. recognition or effects of the title under contemporary French law;
4. documented institutional use of `Baron Mariani` as an identity / *nom d'usage*.

A strong answer to one question does not automatically answer the others.

## Evidence states

- `FACT`
- `STRONG EVIDENCE`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `UNKNOWN`

## 1. Antoine Dominique Mariani — Westphalia, 1813

### STRONG EVIDENCE

Antoine Dominique Mariani is documented by historical secondary sources as having been created baron by Jérôme Bonaparte, King of Westphalia, in 1813.

The Constitution of the Kingdom of Westphalia expressly maintained nobility in its various degrees and qualifications while abolishing the associated exclusive rights, exemptions and privileges.

### UNKNOWN

The primary instrument creating the Mariani title — decree, letters patent or publication in the *Moniteur westphalien* — has not yet been recovered.

The exact rules governing devolution of this particular title have not yet been established from a primary source.

### HYPOTHESIS TO TEST

The applicable devolution may depend on pre-existing German / Holy Roman Empire noble usages preserved by Westphalian law. This must not be stated as fact until the relevant legal sources and the 1813 instrument have been identified.

## 2. Louis-Thomas Mariani — French parliamentary precedent

### FACT

Louis-Thomas Joseph Maurice Jérôme Mariani (1815–1890), son of Antoine Dominique Mariani, sat for Corsica in the French Corps législatif during its second legislature (1857–1863).

Institutional source: Assemblée nationale, Sycomore.

A parliamentary-era photographic portrait preserved by the Musée d'Orsay is catalogued **Baron Mariani**. Its historical mount bears, among other inscriptions:

- `Le corps législatif XII`;
- `Mariani, baron, 1815-1890, Corse, ancien officier`;
- `Bon Mariani, député de la Corse`.

An 1863 electoral protest preserved by the Università di Corsica is signed:

> Baron MARIANI, Ancien député.

### Conclusion

Use of `Baron Mariani` in a French parliamentary and electoral context is documented in the nineteenth century. It is not a contemporary electoral invention.

## 3. Jean Hugues Noël Robert — contemporary institutional use

### 2017 — Ministry of the Interior

### FACT

The official legislative-election results identify the candidate as:

> M. Le BARON MARIANI

### 2024 — Conseil constitutionnel / Journal officiel

### FACT

Decision no. 2024-6309 AN identifies Marie-Louise Robert and Jean Hugues Robert as:

> candidats titulaire et suppléant sous les noms d'usage de baronne et baron MARIANI

The decision was published in the *Journal officiel de la République française* on 1 October 2024.

This establishes, at minimum, official institutional use of `baron MARIANI` as a *nom d'usage*.

It does **not**, by itself, establish recognition by French law of the underlying foreign noble title or its devolution.

## 4. 2026 Senate projection

### FACT

The Senate's 2026 senatorial-election publication uses `Baron MARIANI`.

### RESEARCH QUESTION

To what extent may an institutional information system normalize or truncate a documented *nom d'usage* without altering its faithful representation?

The relevant distinction is:

```text
source identity
→ normalization
→ institutional projection
```

Normalization must not silently be confused with faithful representation.

## 5. Current evidentiary conclusion

### Established

- historical existence of the Baron Mariani title;
- nineteenth-century transmission at least from Antoine Dominique to his son Louis-Thomas in documented public usage;
- parliamentary use of `Baron Mariani` in France;
- official electoral use in 2017;
- explicit qualification `nom d'usage` by the Conseil constitutionnel in 2024.

### Not yet established

- primary 1813 creation instrument;
- complete rules of devolution;
- complete genealogical chain supporting the present holder;
- contemporary French legal recognition of the foreign noble title itself.

These open questions must remain distinct from the independently established institutional-identity question.
