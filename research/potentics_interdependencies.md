# Potentics — Capabilities, Interdependencies, and the Topology of Possibility

## Abstract

Autonomy is often implicitly understood as the reduction of dependency. This paper explores a different hypothesis:

> **Autonomy is not autarky. It is the capacity to govern one's interdependencies.**

Dependencies are neither beneficial nor harmful in themselves. Their effects depend on the wider configuration in which they occur: other dependencies, available substitutes, exit costs, feedback loops, environmental conditions, institutional rules, and the capabilities of the actors involved.

Some interdependencies reduce the space of possibilities through capture, rigidity, opacity, or cascading vulnerability. Others enlarge it by creating capabilities that no participant possesses in isolation.

Potentics studies this transformation of the possible.

The relevant unit of analysis is therefore not necessarily an isolated actor or dependency, but a dynamic configuration of actors, resources, constraints, relationships, and environment.

This paper introduces the working notions of **relational capability**, **capability topology**, and **reconfigurable interdependence**, and connects them to Rational Exploration of Possibilities, the Second Method, the Reality Responds principle, and the Explorer Machine.

## 1. Autonomy Is Not Autarky

A system that depends on nothing outside itself may appear maximally autonomous. In practice, such autarky often comes at the cost of drastically reduced capabilities.

Humans, communities, organizations, software systems, territories, and living ecosystems derive much of their effective capability from relationships.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> How can dependency be eliminated?

but:

> **Which interdependencies increase effective autonomy, under which conditions, and at what risk?**

A chosen dependency can increase autonomy when it provides access to capabilities that would otherwise be unavailable. Conversely, a dependency initially chosen freely may later become capturing.

Autonomy must therefore include not only the ability to choose relationships, but also the capacity to revise, replace, bypass, combine, or terminate them.

## 2. Dependencies Have No Stable Value in Isolation

It is tempting to classify dependencies as beneficial or harmful. This classification is too simple.

Consider a dependency from actor A to provider B. Its consequences depend on surrounding conditions: alternatives, switching costs, portability, hidden common providers, reversibility, failure propagation, compensating dependencies, and environmental change.

The same dependency may therefore be useful in one configuration, dangerous in another, harmless under normal conditions, critical during a disturbance, temporarily beneficial, or progressively capturing.

> **The object of analysis is not the dependency alone. It is the configuration of interdependencies.**

## 3. Relational Capability

A capability does not necessarily belong to a single actor.

Consider a donkey, a human passer-by, and an accessible water trough. The donkey cannot refill the trough. The passer-by, considered independently, has no particular capability or reason to maintain that specific water supply. The trough has no agency.

Yet under suitable conditions:

```text
donkey + human attention + accessible trough + water source
→ effective capability to restore available drinking water
```

This capability exists at the level of the configuration.

> **A relational capability is an effective capability that emerges from a configuration of actors, resources, relationships, and environmental conditions, and cannot be adequately attributed to any component considered in isolation.**

This differs from simple aggregation. The relation itself contributes to the capability.

## 4. Capability Topology

Let a system at time `t` be represented conceptually as:

`G(t) = actors + resources + relationships + constraints + rules + environment`

Let `P(G(t))` represent the set of capabilities and trajectories effectively accessible from that configuration.

A modification of the relational structure, `ΔG`, may cause `ΔP` without changing the intrinsic capabilities of any individual component.

Adding a communication channel may create cooperation. Adding a fence may destroy access. Adding an alternative supplier may transform a capturing dependency into an optional one. Adding a battery may transform dependence on an electrical grid from a single point of failure into one resource among several.

We call the study of these effects **capability topology**:

> **Capability topology studies how the structure and quality of relationships within a system determine which capabilities and trajectories become effectively accessible.**

The term is provisional and must be tested against existing scientific vocabulary before stabilization.

## 5. Reconfigurability

A static map of dependencies is insufficient. Systems change. Providers fail. Institutions evolve. Rules change. Relationships deteriorate or improve. New alternatives appear.

A dependency that was desirable yesterday may become harmful tomorrow.

> **Autonomy depends partly on the capacity to reconfigure the topology of one's interdependencies.**

Important properties include reversibility, substitutability, portability, redundancy, interoperability, diversity, observability, bounded exit cost, and the ability to create new relationships.

This leads to a stronger formulation:

> **Autonomy is not the absence of dependencies, but the effective capacity to govern and reconfigure interdependencies while preserving an acceptable space of possibilities.**

## 6. Exploration Under Uncertainty

The value of a relationship cannot always be known before entering it. Some relationships initially appear unproductive, inconvenient, or costly while opening unexpected capabilities later.

A purely optimization-oriented system tends to reject such relationships because their immediate expected benefit is unclear. Rational Exploration of Possibilities adopts a different posture.

When downside is bounded and reversibility remains high, uncertain relationships may deserve exploration because their **option value** or **information value** can be large.

> **Explore broadly when failure is affordable and the space of potentially opened capabilities is large.**

This is not a rule to accept arbitrary risk. It is a rule against prematurely eliminating poorly understood possibilities.

## 7. Interdependence and Systemic Risk

More connectivity is not necessarily better. Interdependence can produce cascading failures, common-mode dependencies, hidden concentration, lock-in, coordination overhead, conflicting interventions, or loss of local autonomy.

Two apparently independent dependencies may ultimately converge on the same upstream resource. Conversely, two dependencies may compensate for each other and increase resilience.

The relevant question is therefore not how many dependencies exist, but:

> **What topology do they form, and how does that topology behave under changing conditions?**

## 8. Commons as Capability-Producing Configurations

Commons provide an important class of examples. Ostrom's work demonstrates that collective governance cannot be reduced to the opposition between centralized state control and isolated private ownership. Polycentric and locally adapted institutional arrangements can allow participants to govern shared resources effectively.

From a Potentics perspective, commons can additionally be examined as **capability-producing configurations**.

The question becomes not only whether a common resource can be governed sustainably, but:

> **Which capabilities emerge because these actors, rules, resources, and feedback mechanisms have been connected in this particular way?**

This does not imply that commons are intrinsically beneficial. A poorly configured common can also destroy capabilities. Again, topology and governance matter.

## 9. Intellectual Genealogy and Claimed Contribution

The framework developed here sits at the intersection of several established research traditions.

Ashby's cybernetics provides a foundational account of regulation, variety, and the relationship between system structure and adaptive capacity (Ashby, 1956). Ostrom's work demonstrates empirically that robust collective governance can emerge from polycentric and context-dependent institutional configurations (Ostrom, 1990, 2005, 2010). Sen's Capability Approach shifts evaluation from resources formally possessed toward substantive opportunities to act and achieve (Sen, 1985, 1999). Relational theories of autonomy challenge the identification of autonomy with independence (Mackenzie & Stoljar, 2000). Ecological resilience and resilience engineering study how systems preserve, adapt, or extend capacity under disturbance (Holling, 1973; Walker et al., 2004; Woods, 2015). Network science demonstrates that interdependence may create both collective functionality and systemic vulnerability (Buldyrev et al., 2010). Complexity theory warns against explaining system behaviour solely through isolated components (Simon, 1962; Morin, 1977–2004).

### Claimed contribution

Potentics does not claim priority over any of these ideas individually.

The proposed contribution is their integration into a framework explicitly oriented toward the **Rational Exploration of Possibilities**:

> **How do dynamic configurations of actors, capabilities, constraints, resources, and interdependencies transform the space of futures that remain effectively accessible?**

The emphasis moves from describing a system, optimizing a fixed objective, or preserving an existing function toward deliberate, traceable, risk-bounded exploration and transformation of its possibility space.

Whether this constitutes a sufficiently distinct scientific contribution remains itself an open research question and must be tested against existing literature.

## 10. Neighbouring Methods: Inquiry, Effectuation, Real Options, and Open-Ended Search

Dewey's theory of inquiry is an important antecedent for loops in which problematic situations lead to inquiry, intervention, consequences, and revision. Popper provides a foundational account of exposing scientific propositions to possible refutation. Neither should be renamed through new vocabulary without demonstrating an operational distinction.

Entrepreneurial **effectuation**, particularly Sarasvathy's affordable-loss principle, is a close neighbour of the rule to explore uncertain paths when downside is bounded. **Real options** theory similarly provides formal tools for valuing the preservation of future choices under uncertainty.

In artificial intelligence and artificial life, **novelty search**, quality-diversity approaches, and research on open-endedness show how premature objective fixation can suppress stepping stones and discoveries that indirect exploration can reveal.

These traditions create strong prior-art obligations for Potentics. The candidate distinction is not the invention of bounded experimentation, option value, falsification, or open-ended search. It is their possible composition within a persistent, governed exploration of an evolving possibility space, including explicit Continuations, Reality Response, and return-to-corpus learning.

## 11. The Potentics Question

The distinctive question proposed here is:

> **How does a dynamic configuration of interdependencies transform the space of effective capabilities and future possibilities?**

Potentics is therefore interested not only in existing capabilities but in transformations such as:

```text
configuration → capability
relationship → new configuration → new capability
blocker → intervention → reopened trajectory
dependency → capture
alternative dependency → restored optionality
experiment → information → newly visible possibilities
```

The object is not merely resilience. It is the transformation of the possible.

## 12. Rational Exploration of Possibilities

Rational Exploration of Possibilities provides the methodological loop:

```text
possible
→ terrain
→ capabilities / dependencies / blockers
→ candidate paths
→ risk / reversibility / information value
→ minimal experiment
→ confrontation with reality
→ observation
→ correction
→ new space of possibilities
→ continuation
```

The purpose is neither unrestricted experimentation nor optimization against a fixed objective. The purpose is to explore an evolving possibility space while controlling downside and continuously allowing reality to modify the map.

## 13. The Reality Responds Principle

Reasoning alone cannot validate an important possibility. At some point, the chain must expose itself to something it does not control.

> **Reality Responds.**

A model may be coherent. Several artificial agents may agree. A simulation may predict success. None of these is sufficient when the relevant claim concerns the external world.

The exploration must eventually create conditions under which reality can contradict it.

## 14. Rossignol

A **Rossignol** is a device, experiment, observation, stakeholder, measurement, or other mechanism through which reality is given an effective opportunity to answer.

The archetype is Rossignol, a real Corsican donkey. Unlike a purely symbolic model, the animal has genuine skin in the game. If water is unavailable, the consequence is real. If a proposed human interaction is unwanted, the animal can approach, ignore, or avoid it. If an urban arrangement is socially incompatible, conflict appears.

> **A Rossignol is whatever prevents an exploration from becoming self-validating abstraction.**

Vernacular formulation:

> **A Rossignol keeps our feet on the ground.**

## 15. The Explorer Machine

The **Explorer Machine** is the operational form of Rational Exploration of Possibilities. It is not necessarily a computer. It may be human, institutional, computational, hybrid, or distributed.

Its function is:

> **to maintain, enlarge, and make practicable an evolving space of possibilities by identifying blockers, constructing or recombining capabilities, exploring interdependencies, confronting hypotheses with reality, and incorporating the response into subsequent exploration.**

Its canonical loop is:

```text
EXPLORE
→ HYPOTHESIZE
→ MAP CAPABILITIES / DEPENDENCIES / BLOCKERS
→ SELECT A BOUNDED PROBE
→ ROSSIGNOL
→ REALITY RESPONSE
→ UPDATE
→ CONTINUE
```

## 16. The Prevention Machine

The **Prevention Machine** is not necessarily a particular institution. It is a regime in which a system systematically reduces the accessible space of possibilities through opacity, capture, excessive centralization, procedural rigidity, fragmentation, artificial cost, irreversible dependency, suppression of experimentation, destruction or withholding of capabilities, or confusion between an obstacle and an impossibility.

Its characteristic transformation is:

```text
possible
→ blocker
→ rigidity / opacity / capture / cost
→ inaccessible capability
→ closed trajectory
```

An Explorer Machine can become a Prevention Machine. No institution is exploratory by essence. This is why an Explorer Machine must also explore itself.

## 17. A Fundamental Operational Invariant

> **Never confuse an obstacle with an impossibility before the obstacle has been investigated.**

This does not mean that every obstacle must be overcome. An obstacle may be justified. A risk may be excessive. A legal or ethical constraint may properly close a trajectory.

The requirement is epistemic: **the reason for closure must be understood rather than merely inherited.**

## 18. Continuations as Preserved Optionality

An exploration frequently discovers more paths than can be pursued immediately. A path that is not selected now should not automatically disappear from the cognitive system.

A **Continuation** can therefore be understood as the operational memory of a possible that remains available for later exploration.

```text
exploration A
→ discovers B, C, D
→ pursues C
→ preserves B and D as Continuations
```

This connects Rational Exploration of Possibilities to branching search, real options, agenda-based reasoning, durable execution, and continuation mechanisms in computing, while giving Continuations a specific role in preserving cognitive optionality.

Institutional amnesia can itself act as a Prevention Machine: a possible is noticed, not immediately pursued, and then effectively ceases to exist because no durable continuation preserves it.

## 19. From Knowledge to Operation

The Second Method provides the epistemic environment: provenance, versioning, objections, plural cognitive contributions, distinction between fact and hypothesis, human arbitration, preservation of history, and correction rather than doctrinal freezing.

Rational Exploration of Possibilities provides the exploratory process.

Potentics provides the conceptual field.

Reality Responds provides the external correction principle.

Rossignol provides the confrontation mechanism.

The Explorer Machine makes the composition operational.

```text
Second Method
      ↓
Rational Exploration of Possibilities
      ↓
Potentics
      ↓
Explorer Machine
      ↓
bounded experiment
      ↓
Rossignol
      ↓
Reality Responds
      ↓
updated possibility space
      ↺
```

This architecture is descriptive, not dogmatic. Its components emerged historically through practice and remain subject to revision through the Second Method itself. In particular, the architecture must never become a Prevention Machine by turning its own vocabulary into mandatory ontology.

## 20. Research Questions

1. Can relational capabilities be identified independently of the vocabulary proposed here?
2. Can changes in capability topology be represented computationally?
3. How should reversibility and substitutability be measured?
4. When does additional connectivity increase capability, and when does it increase systemic risk?
5. Can an Explorer Machine detect that it is becoming a Prevention Machine?
6. How can hidden common-mode dependencies be discovered?
7. Can the information value of a low-downside exploration be estimated before execution?
8. What constitutes a sufficient Rossignol for different classes of claims?
9. Can Potentics distinguish robust possibilities from merely imaginable ones?
10. Can capability topology be applied consistently across human, ecological, institutional, and computational systems?
11. What, if anything, remains distinctive after comparison with effectuation, real options, open-ended search, inquiry, and existing capability theories?

## 21. Status

This document is a working research artifact. The terminology is intentionally provisional.

In particular, **relational capability** may overlap with established literature; **capability topology** requires terminology and prior-art review; the formal representation `P(G)` is conceptual, not yet a mathematical theory; the relationship between Potentics and existing capability theories requires deeper study; and the Explorer/Prevention duality requires operational metrics.

These are research tasks, not defects to conceal. They are precisely where the exploration should continue.
